# Handy — presentation script

Imagine I'm a homeowner. My faucet leaks, the toilet won't flush, and there's water under the sink. Now my evening is disappearing into searches, calls, and trying to figure out who I can trust.

Research from [Modernize in 2021](https://investor.quinstreet.com/node/16861/pdf) found that 56 percent of homeowners spent one to five hours researching their home-improvement project before even requesting a quote. And that's before the calls and scheduling begin.

For a property manager, those problems arrive across multiple properties: inspect the leak, find a handyman, compare prices, coordinate the tenant and the visit. Then another report comes in, and it starts again.

In comes Handy.

Here's my faucet spraying water sideways. I upload the photo and describe what's happening. Handy gives a preliminary assessment and creates a repair brief.

Now, who should I call? This is where Keewano comes in. Handy uses its history of provider responses, punctuality, and quote accuracy to rank the options. Keewano also helps spot repeat problems at a property, so the agent can ask about replacement as well as repair. For this demo, we're using sample history and test providers.

I tap to request quotes, and Dial takes over the phone calls. Its AI agent explains the repair brief and asks about price, fees, and availability. Dial also sends the repair photo by message to give the provider more context. Handy brings back the quotes, including pricing, fees, and availability, so I can compare my options in one place.

Then I choose a quote and provide times that work for me. Only after I approve does Dial call the provider again to schedule. It asks for a time within my windows and brings back the confirmation. The appointment stays attached to the right property and unit.

Think about what that takes off my plate: researching the problem, explaining it repeatedly, collecting quotes, and chasing a time that works. My part becomes reporting the issue, choosing a quote, and approving a time. Handy handles the back-and-forth. For a property manager, that same benefit repeats across every unit.

To connect these steps, we used Alike to share engineering decisions across our team's coding sessions, helping us build the app and calling service around the same flow. Keewano provides the history, Dial handles the conversations, and Alike helped us build the pieces together.

We propose starting with property managers, where repairs repeat across units, with per-unit subscriptions and booking fees. Then we expand to homeowners.

We estimate Handy could save about a hundred minutes of hands-on coordination per repair. For a property with ten repairs a year, that's roughly seventeen hours back annually.

Whether it's your own home or a property you manage, a repair shouldn't take over your day.

Handy. A photo, an approval, and your time back.
