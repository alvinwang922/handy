# Handy — presentation script

Imagine I'm a new homeowner moving into a house. Before I've finished unpacking, I discover a leaky faucet, a toilet that won't flush, and water under the kitchen sink. Now my evening is disappearing into searches, calls, and trying to figure out who I can trust.

Research from [Modernize in 2021](https://investor.quinstreet.com/node/16861/pdf) found that 56 percent of homeowners spent one to five hours researching their home-improvement project before even requesting a quote. And that's before the calls and scheduling begin.

Or imagine I'm a property manager. The same problems arrive from tenants across multiple properties. I may have to drive out to inspect a leak, find a handyman, compare prices, and coordinate the tenant and the visit. Then another report comes in, and I start again.

In comes Handy.

Here's the leak. I upload a photo and describe what's happening. Claude gives a preliminary assessment and creates a repair brief. If it's a simple fix, Handy offers DIY guidance and part options, with compatibility checks before buying. Here, I'm choosing professional help.

Now Handy has a repair brief to share with providers. Keewano helps rank them by response rate, punctuality, and quote accuracy. It also spots repeat problems at a property, so the agent can ask about replacement as well as repair. For this demo, we're using sample history and test providers.

I tap to request quotes. Dial's AI agent makes the call, explains the issue, and asks about price, fees, and availability. Claude turns the conversation into a quote I can compare. I can see what's included, what might cost extra, and the transcript behind it. I get to make the decision without repeating the same explanation on every call.

Then I choose a quote and provide times that work for me. Only after I approve does Handy call back to schedule. The provider confirms a matching window, and the appointment stays attached to the right property and unit.

We've tested both steps with real Dial calls to our test provider: getting a quote, then calling back to confirm a booking. Dial also delivered the repair photo by message.

Think about what that takes off my plate: researching the problem, explaining it repeatedly, collecting quotes, and chasing a time that works. My part becomes reporting the issue, choosing a quote, and approving a time. Handy handles the back-and-forth. For a property manager, that same benefit repeats across every unit.

Behind the scenes, Alike helped us share engineering decisions across our coding sessions. We also used Sparta Worlds to test how a separate prototype for future deposit refunds handles service failures.

We propose starting with property managers, where repairs repeat across units, with per-unit subscriptions and booking fees. Then we expand to homeowners.

Whether it's your own home or a property you manage, a repair shouldn't take over your day.

Handy. A photo, an approval, and your time back.
